## Frequently Asked Questions

**1. Is this compliant with Google's review policies?**

Yes. Google allows businesses to ask customers for reviews. What it doesn't allow is paying for reviews, writing fake reviews, or only asking happy customers while blocking unhappy ones. Our system follows those rules: every customer who completes a job gets the same request and can reach your Google review page, and we never offer incentives in exchange for reviews. The private feedback option gives customers an extra way to reach you directly. It doesn't replace their ability to post publicly. Every review you get is real and comes from a real customer.

**2. How long does setup take?**

Your part takes under 10 minutes. On a short onboarding call, we connect your job or invoicing software, confirm your branding, and approve your message wording. We handle the rest, and most clients are sending their first review requests within [48 hours].

**3. Do my staff need to learn new software?**

No. Your team keeps working the way they do now. When they mark a job complete or send an invoice in your current software, the review request goes out on its own. If you'd like to see your reviews and feedback, you can use a simple mobile app, but it's optional. Most owners just read the text alerts.

**4. What if a customer has a bad experience?**

You'll want to hear about it before anyone else does. When a customer tells us something went wrong, they're offered a short private form that goes straight to you, and you get an instant alert with their name and number. That gives you the chance to call, fix the problem, and often turn an upset customer into a loyal one. The customer can still leave a public Google review if they choose. In our experience, a fast, personal response is the best way to keep a bad day from turning into a bad review.

**5. How does the custom website build work alongside the review software?**

Both run on the same platform, so they're connected from day one. New Google reviews appear on your website automatically. Website chats, quote requests, and booked appointments land in the same inbox as your review alerts. And every customer who books through your site is automatically added to your review follow-up once the job is done. You can start with reviews and add a website later, or bundle both from the start. We'll recommend whichever makes sense for your business on the demo call.

## Notes Before You Publish

1. **Set up the feedback step compliantly.** In your GoHighLevel workflow, don't send low-rating customers *only* to a private form. Show the Google review link to everyone, and offer the private form alongside it. Google's policy ([support.google.com/business/answer/7400114](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7400114)) bans "selectively soliciting positive reviews", and the FTC's 2024 rule on consumer reviews targets review suppression. Gating can get reviews removed or a profile suspended, and that is the worst possible outcome for a review agency's client.
2. **Don't use unsourced statistics.** The brief mentioned "95% of happy customers forget to leave feedback." I left the figure out because there's no reliable source for it. If you find a study you trust (e.g., BrightLocal's annual Local Consumer Review Survey), quote it with a citation.
3. **Don't promise rankings.** The copy says reviews are *a signal* that helps rankings, which is accurate. Avoid "#1 on Google guaranteed" wording in ads and sales calls.
4. **SMS compliance (A2P 10DLC).** Register your brand and campaign in GoHighLevel's LC Phone before sending. Customers must have given their number as part of doing business with your client, and every message needs opt-out language.
5. **Replace every `[bracket]`**, especially prices, integration names, and the setup-time promise, with numbers you can deliver consistently.
6. **Testimonials must be real.** Use the "Founding Client" block until you have them.
